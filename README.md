## Background
Over the past decade, Europe has faced significant energy challenges related to production and consumption, driven by climate goals, geopolitical tensions, and shifting energy demands. The transition to renewable energy has accelerated, with increased investment in wind, solar, and hydropower. However, reliance on fossil fuels, particularly natural gas, has highlighted Europe's dependency on external energy sources.
Energy consumption patterns have also changed, with efforts to improve energy efficiency in industries, transport, and households. The push for electrification, particularly in transport and heating, has increased demand, requiring grid modernization and better energy storage solutions.
Geographic information and statistics play a crucial role in managing these challenges. Spatial data helps map renewable energy potential, plan infrastructure, and optimize energy distribution. Statistics on consumption and production inform policy decisions, ensuring energy security and sustainability. Without accurate geographic and statistical data, balancing supply, demand, and environmental concerns would be significantly more difficult.
Objectives: This work package aims to enhance the integration of geospatial and statistical data for energy statistics in support of EU climate and energy policies and the UN Sustainable Development Goals (notably SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy, and SDG 13: Climate Action). The activities aim at develop new methodologies and data infrastructures that improve the spatial granularity, relevance, and interoperability of energy-related statistics. It will build on existing data sources, national registers, and GIS applications already in operational use in Norway.
Rationale and Policy Relevance:
Accurate, location-based energy statistics are crucial for monitoring the transition to renewable energy, understanding spatial disparities in energy access and consumption, and planning infrastructure investments. This WP directly supports:
- The EU’s Green Deal and REPowerEU strategy,
- The Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD),
- The Renewable Energy Directive (RED II and RED III),
- The High Value Dataset Directive, especially for energy, buildings, and administrative units.
By linking official energy statistics with geospatial information, the WP will support evidence-based decisions on local and regional energy transitions and enable improved modeling and forecasting at various spatial levels.

## Scope and Activities:
The WP will map and assess existing energy data sources in Norway relevant to residential and industrial energy consumption, electricity production, district heating, and energy infrastructure. Key sources include:
- SSB’s annual and monthly energy statistics
- Energy certificates (Enova/NEB)
- Grid infrastructure data from the Norwegian Water Resources and Energy 
Directorate (NVE)
- Building and land use data from the cadastre and building register (Matrikkel)
- Address and administrative units from the Norwegian Mapping Authority
Develop and test geospatial linking methodologies for integrating energy data at sub-national levels using standard geographies (e.g., municipalities, 1 km grids, or hexagonal units).
Produce enhanced spatial indicators related to energy efficiency, consumption and production, for example:

- Electricity consumption per capita by area
- Renewable production capacity per km²
- Energy performance of buildings by region
- Support harmonization and interoperability through metadata documentation, adoption of INSPIRE-compliant standards, and alignment with GSGF principles.
- Pilot a cross-domain use case, e.g., linking energy data with socio-economic indicators (household income, building age, or urban form), enabling identification of areas with high energy poverty risk.
- Ensure reusability and dissemination through the publication of open datasets (where permitted), sharing of tools/methodologies, and participation in EFGS/GISCO exchanges.

## Expected Outcomes
- A reproducible, scalable framework for the integration of geospatial and statistical energy data.
- Enhanced statistical outputs that reflect local and regional variations in energy consumption and supply.
- Improved input to SDG reporting, EU energy policy monitoring, and climate adaptation planning.
- Documentation and metadata enabling adoption in other NSIs or EU countries.

## Contribution to Overall Project Goals:
This WP contributes directly to the Eurostat call’s objectives by demonstrating cross-domain integration (energy + geospatial), fostering collaboration with mapping authorities, and delivering innovative, sustainable solutions with EU policy relevance. It will also showcase the potential of Earth observation data (e.g., land cover, solar potential) to complement statistical energy data.
